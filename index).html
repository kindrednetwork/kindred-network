Who We Are

More than a volunteer program.

The Kindred Network is a community-driven volunteer and cultural exchange project rooted in Busua, Ghana. Volunteers live, surf, and connect in Busua — but our work reaches further. We operate across Dixcove, Agona, and the surrounding coastal villages of Ghana's Western Region — communities full of potential that bigger programs simply walk past.

Whether you're on a gap year, a long holiday, or simply looking for something meaningful, we bring together people from all backgrounds to build friendships, surf waves, share stories, and make a difference in communities that truly need it — and will never forget you were there.

A Typical Day

What your days will look like

Every day is different — but here's the kind of rhythm you can expect as a Kindred Network volunteer.

🌅

Morning · 7:00am – 9:00am

Wake Up & Breakfast

Start your day with a fresh Ghanaian breakfast — think fried eggs, bread, Milo, or local porridge. Fuel up and get ready for the day ahead.

🚐

Morning · 9:00am – 9:30am

Transport to Placement

Transport from Busua to your placement in Dixcove, Agona or a nearby village is provided. A short scenic ride along the coast and you arrive ready to make an impact.

💪

Morning · 9:30am – 12:30pm

Volunteering & Placement Work

The heart of your day — teaching in classrooms, caring for children, supporting medical staff at the hospital, or running surf and culture sessions on Busua Beach. A focused morning of real, meaningful work that finishes at 12:30pm.

🌊

Afternoon · 12:30pm – 6:00pm

Return to Busua · Free Time

After a fulfilling morning of volunteering, the rest of the afternoon is yours. Head to the beach, swim, surf, explore the village, nap in a hammock, or simply soak in the Busua vibe. You've earned it.

🍛

Evening · 6:00pm – 7:30pm

Dinner Together

Share a hot Ghanaian dinner with your fellow volunteers. Jollof rice, waakye, banku, fresh fish — real food, real conversation, real connection.

Night · 7:30pm onwards

Bonfire on Busua Beach 🌊

The moment that makes Kindred Network different from every other program. Gather around a bonfire on the beach while a community elder of Busua shares the history, stories, and soul of this place. The waves behind you. The stars above. The fire in front. This is the memory you'll carry home forever.

Weekends are free for personal exploration, trips, or rest. Optional group activities may be organised — these are always communicated in advance.

Got Questions?

Everything you need to know

We believe in full transparency. Here are the questions we get asked most — answered honestly.

Is Ghana safe for solo travelers?

+

Ghana is one of the safest and most welcoming countries in West Africa. It has a long history of political stability and is widely regarded as one of Africa's most peaceful nations. Busua itself is a small, tight-knit coastal village where everybody knows everybody. You will be known, looked after, and welcomed as part of the community from day one. You won't be a stranger — you'll be a guest that the whole village keeps an eye on. Our team is also on the ground 24/7 and will always be reachable.

Do I need any experience to volunteer?

+

No experience needed at all. What matters most is your attitude — showing up with energy, openness, and a genuine desire to connect. Our local team and the teachers, medical staff, and community members at each placement will guide you every step of the way. Whether it's your first time volunteering or your tenth, you will be supported from day one. The most impactful volunteers we've seen are not always the most qualified — they're the ones who show up with heart.

What language do people speak in Busua?

+

The official language of Ghana is English, so you will be able to communicate at your placements and in the community. In Busua and the surrounding villages, people speak Ahanta and Fante as their first languages, so you may find that some locals — especially children — have basic English. But don't worry — language has never stopped real human connection. A smile, some effort, and a few words of Fante will take you further than you think. Our local team will always be there to help bridge any gaps.

What should I pack?

+

Busua is warm and humid year round so pack light and breathable clothing. Here's what we recommend:

  • 👕 Light, modest clothing for school and hospital placements
  • 🩱 Swimwear and a rash guard for the beach and surfing
  • 👟 Comfortable walking shoes and flip flops
  • 🌧️ A light rain jacket — Ghana has a rainy season
  • 🧴 Sunscreen and insect repellent
  • 🎒 A daypack for your daily commute to placements

Leave the heavy stuff at home — Busua is a simple, beautiful beach village. The lighter you pack the better!

How do I get from Accra to Busua?

+

Don't worry about this at all. From the moment you land at Accra Kotoka International Airport, we've got you. Either Anthony himself or a member of our team will be there to pick you up and drive you all the way to Busua. No taxis, no confusion, no stressful arrival. Just show up and we handle the rest.

Is there WiFi in Busua?

+

WiFi is not widely available in Busua but don't let that stop you — when you arrive our team will help you get a local Ghanaian SIM card which are very affordable and will keep you connected throughout your stay. Consider it a digital detox with an option to stay in touch when you need to!

What is the food like?

+

The food in Busua is something else entirely. Fresh, local and full of flavour — think jollof rice, waakye, banku, fresh grilled fish straight from the ocean and so much more. Every week volunteers get to vote on what they want to eat — the whole group decides together and the winning dish is what's on the menu. It's one of the small things that makes the Kindred Network feel like a real community.

What happens on weekends and holidays?

+

Weekends and holidays are yours to enjoy. We organise beach activities including volleyball and football right on Busua Beach — joining locals and making memories that go way beyond volunteering. No schedules, no pressure. Just good people, good vibes and the best beach in Ghana.
